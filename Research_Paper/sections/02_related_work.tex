\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Agentic Environmental AI Frameworks}
The integration of artificial intelligence into environmental monitoring and disaster management has witnessed a paradigm shift toward multi-agent systems (MAS). Recent frameworks can be organized into four distinct architectural paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{LLM-Chained Copilots} \cite{talemi2026llm, cantonjos2026agentic}: Systems utilizing Large Language Models to summarize natural language bulletins and prompt emergency managers. While highly expressive, they suffer from hallucination and lack numerical calibration under sensor failure.
    \item \textit{Remote-Sensing Tool-Use Agents} \cite{jiang2026floodllm, sandeep2026remote}: Autonomous agents executing programmatic API calls to extract satellite spectral indices. These systems rely on heuristic decision trees to resolve conflicting satellite passes.
    \item \textit{Knowledge-Graph Wrappers} \cite{lee2025knowledge, sayyad2025multi}: Graph neural network wrappers mapping geospatial sensors to domain ontologies. They excel at static spatial relationships but struggle with high-frequency dynamic weather shifts.
    \item \textit{MAS Orchestrations} \cite{hofmeister2023multi, hofmeister2024uncertainty, zadeh2025evidential, shabbir2025multimodal}: Distributed agent ensembles coordinating environmental sensor nodes.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Subjective Logic and Evidential Fusion in MAS}
Subjective Logic (SL), formulated by J{\o}sang \cite{josang2016subjective}, extends Dempster-Shafer belief theory by establishing a rigorous mathematical bijection between Dirichlet evidence distributions and evidential opinions $\omega = (\mathbf{b}, u, \mathbf{a})$. Unlike traditional Bayesian networks that collapse uncertainty into point probability estimates, Subjective Logic explicitly separates belief mass $\mathbf{b}$ from unobserved epistemic uncertainty $u$.

In multi-agent systems, fusing opinions from multiple agents requires associative operators. van der Heijden et al. \cite{heijden2018distributed} introduced Cumulative Belief Fusion (CBF), Weighted Belief Fusion (WBF), and Consensus \& Compromise Fusion (CCF) to resolve agent agreement. Kaplan et al. \cite{kaplan2015subjective} extended SL to information security management systems by formalizing partial observability projections under missing data streams. Wang and Singh \cite{wang2007formal} established Brier-score reputation tracking for agent trust update, while Nielsen and Parsons \cite{nielsen2006argumentation} and Sun et al. \cite{sun2024targeted} explored argumentation-driven multi-agent communication.

\subsection{Conflict Resolution in Multi-Agent Systems}
Resolving conflicting sensory inputs across heterogeneous agents remains a core challenge in multi-agent coordination. Chikoti \cite{chikoti2025argumentation} investigated argumentation-based consensus protocols for environmental sensor networks, showing that rule-based argumentation requires extensive manual tuning. In contrast, Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence offers a non-parametric, distance-based measure of opinion conflict \cite{heijden2018distributed}, enabling dynamic switching between consensus and weighted fusion operators.

\subsection{Evidential Deep Learning}
Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) \cite{liu2025evidential, chen2025deep, li2024subjective} parameterizes neural networks to output the parameters of a Dirichlet distribution directly. While EDL has been applied to spatial prediction under distribution shift \cite{liu2025evidential} and hydrological forecasting \cite{chen2025deep}, training end-to-end EDL models on small spatio-temporal datasets risks severe overfitting and severe computational overhead on CPU hardware. AEGIS avoids these pitfalls by adopting a \textit{Hybrid Evidential Head} that concatenates low-dimensional Subjective Logic opinion tensors with raw multi-modal features, combining the efficiency of gradient-boosted decision trees (LightGBM) with the mathematical guarantees of Subjective Logic.
